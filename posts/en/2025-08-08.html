Internship at AWS DSQL: My Experience

August 8th, 2025

Introduction

On Friday, August 8th, 2025, I finished my internship at Amazon Web Services. It was an incredible experience, and I want to share it with all of you. Honestly, the past year has been full of life-changing events for me. I earned my undergraduate degree in Computer Science last December and started my Master's degree this January. Not too long afterwards, I interviewed with DSQL this March and started working this May. After 12 weeks of internship, my manager gave me an "inclined" vote to return to DSQL. I could not be more thrilled! Anyways, enough rambling in this section. Let's talk about my experience interning at AWS DSQL!

The DSQL project and the DPFE team

The team I interned with is a part of the Aurora DSQL project, a serverless distributed relational database written in Rust. I joined 2 days before launch, and was present in the org's launch photoshoot even though I had not contributed anything to the project.

I was a part of the Data Plane Front-end team. Despite the name, we do not do front-end as in user interfaces or UX designs and such. The DPFE team maintains software that facilitates clients' connection to DSQL's PostgreSQL engine. I'm not sure how much I'm allowed to say here before it's considered leaking confidential design. Thus, I'm going to keep it as vague as possible and not go any lower-level than what DSQL has on their website.

If you look at the diagram above, the DPFE team operates somewhere between the customer's application and the query processing layer. There are a lot of intricacies going on under the hood to ensure lightning-fast connection time and 99.99% availability. It's also the first time I get to work with Rust's asynchronous crate, tokio. Interesting stuff!

My internship project

My project supported our sister team that works on the query processing layer. At a high level, I refactored components in DPFE to make it easier for developers to experiment with different build permutations of the query processing software in real infrastructure. I also created a CLI tool to interact with this feature, along with some extra cloud development work. This meant I had to ramp up on asynchronous Rust and navigate DPFE's large, complex codebase, something that was overwhelming at first. During the first month, I experienced plenty of impostor syndrome, wondering if I was really cut out to be a systems engineer.

Phases of my internship

The first two weeks were onboarding and ramping up on asynchronous Rust. I was doing training and getting to know Amazon's internal development tools. I also met with team members and did standard onboarding stuff. I started to do the Rustlings exercises to get refreshed on Rust (I highly recommend it if you want to learn Rust). I was then given a Rust exercise to implement a simple server-client application using Rust's tokio crate.

By the second sprint, the real work started to speed up. I wrote the low-level design document and have it reviewed with the team by week 3. I submitted my first pull request to the mainline by week 4. By that time, I'm starting to get familiar with the code base and how each of the DPFE's components interacts with each other. Still, there was a lot more to learn. By week 7, I have done all the necessary refactoring of DPFE's components to support my project. All that's left is to make the CLI. I also had my midpoint evaluation around this time, and I was given feedback from my manager and mentor. There were areas where I could improve, and I tried my best to do better during the second half of the internship.

And by week 9, everything was pretty much done. I informed our sister team about the project, and several developers started to use it for their testing. I received feedback and feature requests, and I spent the rest of the time working on the stretch goals and optimizing my core project.

I demoed my project in week 10 to the entire DSQL organization. I was very nervous, as I am not much of a public speaker. Still, I pulled through, and everyone seemed to like what I had done. The director of DSQL gave me his compliments, which, to be honest, felt very good. During the final two weeks, I finished the stretch goal, wrote the knowledge transfer document, and fixed some permission issues with the project. I'm proud to say that I have left no unfinished tasks for my team to pick up!

The result

Well, I had a very successful internship. It seems my work was satisfactory, and I managed to pick up and complete more work as well. I was proud of myself. I went from being confused and doubting myself to being slightly less confused and having a small sense of accomplishment. During the final meeting, I was given an "inclined" vote from my manager to return to DSQL. If things go well, I will update you when I sign the full-time offer!

Some final ramblings and conclusion

I want to talk a bit more about things that are going on within the software engineering world of Big Tech. First of all, with the recent release of GPT-5, I want to touch on the use of AI and how it has impacted our productivity. The consensus from my team was that you can trust it about 50% of the time. The fact is, AI is still pretty bad at asynchronous, concurrent, and parallel programming, all of which require real-world context that LLMs can't really grasp. Also, Rust is a fairly new language with less data to train on than, say, Python or JavaScript. At the end of the day, I find it useful for summarizing a new repository or prototyping, general lazy thinking work, where you know it can do decently. I also ask AI to generate bash commands for me when I'm too lazy to look up the manual. It's safe to say that AI won't be replacing system engineers soon.

I want to give a huge shout-out and thank you to each and every member of my team, and especially my mentor, Ben. The bar for the DPFE team is really high, and all of them are very talented engineers. Ben was especially helpful, and I felt thoroughly supported during the entire duration of the internship. Thanks again, Ben!

I'm excited to return to DSQL after I finish my Master's degree. Meanwhile, I'm taking 3 classes in C next semester, and I'm pretty excited about that! This post is getting long now, so I'll have to end it here. See ya!
