GrapheneOS: Digital Privacy Meets Security

July 31st, 2025

Introduction

Your phone is a tracking device. No, seriously, it collects and sends your data, including what you say, your location, your usage data, and a lot more personal information to Big Tech. Google is the most egregious about this, and since Android is the most popular mobile device operating system, many people are getting their data harvested and used by Google. And before you jump to Apple devices and iOS, Apple is almost just as bad.

In a world where a smartphone is almost essential, it seems that there is no escape from Big Tech's data collection. But wait, there is!

I have been using GrapheneOS for about 2 months now. After a long 5 years of service, I retired my Samsung, got myself a Google Pixel, and installed GrapheneOS on it. I know, I know, getting a Google-branded phone after writing a whole paragraph criticizing the company has its layers of irony, but there is a very specific reason for this, so bear with me here.

What is GrapheneOS? How is it different from Android?

GrapheneOS is a security-focused mobile operating system. It is based on the Android Open Source Project (AOSP), which is pure Android code without any Google apps or proprietary services, and it's fully open source.

Ever noticed how, when you get a new phone, certain proprietary apps like Chrome, Gmail, and YouTube are pre-installed? If you use iOS, equivalent examples would be Safari, Mail, and Apple TV. These apps cannot be uninstalled, and they have heightened privileges to access your data and peripherals, such as the microphone and camera.

GrapheneOS doesn't ship with these pre-installed proprietary apps. It still gives you the option to install them if you want via Sandboxed Google Play. In essence, it treats Google apps and services like how Android treats other third-party apps: running in a sandboxed environment with restricted permissions to your data and peripherals unless explicitly granted otherwise.

Security enhancements

Aside from being shipped de-Googled, GrapheneOS also has some other enhanced security features, such as extensive memory safety hardening techniques to prevent common vulnerabilities like buffer overflows and use-after-free bugs.

It also provides stricter app sandboxing with enhanced permission controls, such as the ability to restrict network access and sensor usage on a per-app basis.

Why a Google phone?

GrapheneOS must be installed exclusively on Google Pixel devices because these phones offer the necessary hardware-backed security features, such as the Titan M secure element and verified boot. Pixels also receive timely security updates and have well-documented, open-source firmware.

There is a bit of irony here in needing Google hardware to break free from its software and tracking, but at the end of the day, it's not the hardware that collects your data.

Installation

If you are still reading this, then I must have captured your attention. You can see how to install GrapheneOS on your Pixel phone here using their web installer. It is very simple and should not take you more than 10 minutes!

Installing apps on GrapheneOS

GrapheneOS supports a vast majority of apps you find on the Google Play Store. As mentioned before, you can still install apps via the Sandboxed Google Play Store. I have Firefox as the main browser, Thunderbird as my email client, Here We Go for maps, and some messaging and banking apps.

You can sort of see the trend I'm going for here: not using apps from Google as well, including their browser, maps, email client, etc. You certainly don't have to do what I do, and running these Google apps with standard sandboxed permissions should be plenty good enough to prevent them from having access to your other data. I should mention that NFC payment with Google Wallet does not work at all, not because of GrapheneOS, but because of arbitrary restrictions placed by Google. If your banking app supports NFC payment, you can use it with no issue.

Ultimately, GrapheneOS only provides security on an operating system level. If you install invasive apps such as Spotify or TikTok, your data will still be sent to these companies. I'm not here to tell you which apps you should or should not use, but reducing the amount of personal data collected overall is a good thing. Google loses broad access to your data, and that is what we are trying to achieve here.

Conclusion

Do I recommend GrapheneOS for everyone? Yes, absolutely. There are few to no downsides to daily driving with GrapheneOS due to vast app support, unless you really, really need Google Wallet's NFC payment.

GrapheneOS lets you use the tools you need while cutting out unnecessary surveillance and minimizing your data exposure. You don't have to abandon your smartphone or limit yourself to a bare minimum of apps.

If you care even a little about who has access to your personal information, GrapheneOS is one of the most powerful steps you can take today.
